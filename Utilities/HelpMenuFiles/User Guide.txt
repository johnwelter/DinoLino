GLOBAL METHODS:

Undo:	
Undoes the last action performed by the user. This returns the metadata of the previous calculation and removes the last drawn operation.

Redo:	
Redoes the last action that was undone by the user. This returns the metadata of the next calculation and redraws the operation.

Clear:	
Clears all drawn operations and resets the metadata to its initial state.

Zoom:
Use the scroll wheel to zoom in and out of the image at the location of the cursor.

Drag image:
Hold Ctrl and left click to drag and move the viewable window while zoomed in.

CURVATURE MODE: 

Geometric calculations to calculate curvature 

----- Circular Arc -----

Approximate a curve as the arc of a circle using three points. 
Click twice to draw a line from each end of the curve (your 'chord'), then click a third time in the midpoint of the curve to draw the chord bisector (your 'sagitta'). 
Click again after arc completion to begin another operation.

Central Angle: A measure of curvature. The central angle of a circular arc drawn using three points. 
Chord-Arc Ratio: A measure of curvature. The length of the first line drawn (chord) divided by arc length.
Rise-Span ratio: Length of the first line drawn (chord) divided by the length of its bisector (sagitta).

----- Parabolic Arc -----

Approximate a curve as a parabola using three points.
Click twice to draw a line from each end of the curve (your 'chord'), then click a third time in the midpoint of the curve to draw the chord bisector (your 'sagitta').
Click again after arc completion to begin another operation.

Chord-Arc Ratio: A measure of curvature. The length of the first line drawn (chord) divided by arc length.
Rise-Span Ratio: Length of the first line drawn (chord) divided by the length of its bisector (sagitta).
Vertex Curvature: The slope (m) of the parabolic arc (y=mx^2...)

----- n-Point Spline -----

Click any amount of times along the curve, and finalize by pressing 'Enter'. This creates an n-point spline. Clicking again after spline completion begins another operation.

Catmull-Rom splines: The spline must pass through every point. This operation uses centripetal Catmull-Rom splines, which are well-suited for non-uniform biological shapes. 
Bezier splines: The spline is pulled towards the clicked points, but does not necessarily pass through them. This operation uses Schneider's Bezier fitting to convert n points into one or more smooth cubic Bezier segments. 

Turn.Angles/Length: A measure of curvature. The sum of the turning angles (change in angle of the tangent lines along the spline) divided by the length of the spline. Approximates the average change in angle of the tangent lines along the spline.
Chord-Arc Ratio: A measure of curvature. The length of the straight line (chord) connecting the start and end points of the curve divided by the length of the spline.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRIANGLE MODE:

Draws a triangle on the image and returns its three interior angles and proportions. 

Click three times anywhere on the image to create a triangle. Click again to begin drawing another triangle.

Angle A:		The angle at vertex A.
Angle B:		The angle at vertex B.
Angle C:		The angle at vertex C.
Aspect Ratio:	Length of the longest side of the triangle divided by its height.
Relative Size:	Area of the current triangle divided by the area of the previous triangle.

Note: Triangle height is the perpendicular distance from its longest side to the opposite vertex.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
DRAW MODE:

Draw basic shapes and lines on the image and return their relative proportions.

----- Draw Shapes -----
Click once to begin drawing a geometric shape, drag to determine size and shape, and click a second time to finish.

Aspect Ratio: Width of the drawn shape divided by its height. NA for lines and angles.
Relative Size: Area of the drawn shape divided by the area of the previous shape. NA for lines and angles.

----- Draw Lines -----
Click twice anywhere on the image to draw a straight line between two points.
For angles, type the desired angle in the text box. Click twice anywhere on the image draw line AB. Line BC will be constrained to the specified angle. Click a third time to finish.

Line Ratio: Length of the drawn line divided by the length of the previous line. NA for shapes.

OUTLINE MODE:

Click objects to generate an enclosed outline around them.

----- Draw -----
Default: 
Faster than Watershed, but may perform poorly for complex images (e.g., multi-toned, patterned, non-uniform background).
Well-suited for geometrically simple objects on a solid background.

Fill Sensitivity:
For objects on a solid background, set fill sensitivity to the maximum value. 
Higher fill sensitivity values allows greater variation of colors and shades within the outlined object, but also increases chances of capturing background on non-uniform backgrounds.

Multi-Click:
Click multiple times to expand the outline. 
Click outside of the region bounded by a dashed line to expand the outline to include that region. 
Click inside the region bounded by a dashed line to finalize the outline.

Watershed:
Higher computational expense leads to slower execution, but it tends to yield a more accurate outline.
Better-suited for complex images (e.g., multi-toned objects, patterned objects, and/or objects on a non-uniform background).

Tip: If the outline is not accurate, try adjusting the fill sensitivity, or switch between default and watershed methods.
Tip: To reduce computational expense and increase performance speed, try cropping or decimating the image.

----- Erase -----
Click, hold the left mouse button, and drag anywhere on the outline to erase that part. Set the eraser size using the slider.
Alternately, hold the 'Alt' button and drag the cursor to erase part of the outline. This may be more convenient when using a trackpad.

----- Push -----
Click, hold the left mouse button, and drag anywhere on the outline to push that part outward. Set the brush size using the slider.
Alternately, hold the 'Alt' button and drag the cursor to erase part of the outline. This may be more convenient when using a trackpad.

----- Smooth -----
Smooth a noisy outline to the desired amount by adjusting the slider value. The higher the number of passes, the more smoothing will occur.
Global smooth smooths the entire outline, while local smooth smooths only the area around the cursor.
For local smooth, set the brush size using the slider and the desired strength. Click, hold the left mouse button, and drag anywhere on the outline to smooth that part. 
Alternately, hold the 'Alt' button and drag the cursor to erase part of the outline. This may be more convenient when using a trackpad.

----- Generate Metadata -----
Aspect Ratio: Width of the outline (its longest side) divided by its height (maximum perpendicular distance from the width).
Perimeter / Area: Length of the outline divided by its area.
Circularity: 4π*Area / Perimeter^2
Solidity: Area of the outlined shape / Convex hull area
Turn.Angles/Length: A measure of curvature. The sum of the turning angles (change in angle of the tangent lines along the outline) divided by the outline perimeter length. Approximates the average change in angle of the tangent lines along the outline.

Elliptic Fourier Analysis: Click to open a pop-up window to perform Elliptic Fourier Analysis (EFA) on the outline.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FILE:

----- Open Image ----- 
Open a 2D image file to analyze. Supported formats include .jpg, .png, .tif, and .bmp. 

----- Open 3D Model (.ply) ----- 
Open a 3D file to analyze. PLY, OBJ, and STL files are supported. 
Position the model by pressing the scroll wheel and dragging, or by pressing the arrow keys. 
Click 'Capture View' to finish positioning the object. This will save the view as a 2D image, which the user can begin measuring.

Note: DinoLino is currently limited to 2D morphometrics. Measurements may only be taken on 2D projections of the 3D model.

----- Import Folder -----
Import a folder of images to analyze. Incompatible file types will be ignored.
Folders that contain several large files may take several minutes to import.

----- Take Screenshot ----- 
Take a screenshot of the current software image screen. The screenshot may be saved as a .jpg, .png, or .tif file. The screenshot will include all drawn elements. 

----- Export Operation History -----
Export the history of all operations performed. Undone or cleared operations will not be included. The exported file will be a workbook in .xlsx format, with each operation's history on a separate sheet.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EDIT:

----- Undo ----- 
Undoes the last operation performed. This removes the drawn elements and data associated with that operation. It will not be stored in the operation history.

----- Redo ----- 
Redoes the most recently undone operation. This reinstates the drawn elements and data associated with that operation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOOLS:

----- Set Scale -----
Click on two points in the image to draw a line. Enter the real-world length of the line in the input box and click "OK" to set the scale. 
This will allow the software to convert pixel measurements to real-world units.

----- Picture Corrections ----- 
Adjust brightness, contrast, or saturation of the uploaded image. Zero is the default value for all parameters.

----- Decimate -----
Decimate the image by a specified factor to reduce its resolution and computational expense. This may be helpful for large images or when using the watershed method in outline mode.

----- Flip -----
Flip image horizontally or vertically to produce a mirror image.

----- Rotate -----
Rotate image by 90 degrees clockwise or counterclockwise.

----- Reposition 3D Object -----
Return to the 3D image viewer after capturing view of a 3D image to reposition the object. Click 'Capture View' when ready to save the new view as a 2D image, which the user can begin measuring.

----- Clear Image Cache -----
Remove all images from the cache without removing associated measurements.

----- Edit Image Cache -----
Review all cached images and remove them one at a time, without removing associated measurements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VIEW:

Adjust various properties using the View drop-down menu items.

----- See Tips ----- 
Toggle on or off to see advice for using the program displayed at the bottom of the screen. Guidelines are also available in the user guide.

----- See Previous Operations ----- 
Toggle on or off to keep drawn elements of previous operations on the screen.

----- See Operation Count -----
View the number of operations performed for each specimen. 
Toggle on to display an on-screen window which contains the number of circular arcs, parabolic arcs, splines, triangles, lines, and outlines drawn for a given specimen.
Opening a new specimen resets count to zero for all operations. Undo and Redo also affect operation count.
The Operation Count window may be dragged anywhere in the workspace.

----- See Navigation Window -----
Toggle on or off to display a navigation window in the top left corner of the screen. 
The navigation window shows a zoomed-out view of the image, with a rectangle indicating the current viewable area. 
While zoomed in, click anywhere in the navigation window to move the viewable area to that location.

----- See Batch Workshop -----
Edit and export data tables as CSV files for each mode. Toggle on or off to display on the right sidebar.

Column names in these data tables correspond to the following: 

"Specimen" = Specimen Name
"Attempt" = Operation Count

In Curvature Mode:
"circ_centangle" = Central Angle of Circular Arc
"circ_chordarc" = Chord-Arc Ratio of Circular Arc
"circ_risespan" = Rise-Span Ratio of Circular Arc
"para_chordarc" = Chord-Arc Ratio of Parabolic Arc
"para_risespan" = Rise-Span Ratio of Parabolic Arc
"para_vertcurv" = Vertex Curvature of Parabolic Arc
"spline_turnangle" = Turning Angle / Spline Length Ratio of n-Point Spline
"spline_chordarc" = Chord-Arc Ratio of n-Point Spline
"spline_length" = Length of n-Point Spline

In Triangle Mode:
"tri_anglea" = Angle A of Triangle
"tri_angleb" = Angle B of Triangle
"tri_anglec" = Angle C of Triangle
"tri_aspect" = Aspect Ratio of Triangle
"tri_area" = Area of Triangle
"tri_relarea" = Relative Area of Triangle (compared to previous)

In Draw Mode:
"rect_aspect" = Aspect Ratio of Rectangle
"rect_area" = Area of Rectangle
"sqr_area" = Area of Square
"ellipse_aspect" = Aspect Ratio of Ellipse
"ellipse_area" = Area of Ellipse
"circ_area" = Area of Circle
"line_length" = Line Length
"line_ratio" = Ratio of line length relative to previous line length
"line_angle" = Angle of line relative to previous line

In Outline Mode:
"outline_aspect" = Outline Aspect Ratio
"outline_perim" = Outline Perimeter Length
"outline_area" = "Area of Filled Outline
"outline_perimarea" = Ratio of Outline Perimeter Length over Outline Filled Area
"outline_circ" = Outline Circularity
"outline_solidity" = Outline Solidity
"outline_turnlength" = Ratio of Sum of Turning Angles / Outline Perimeter Length

EFA Data:
The coefficients table may be exported in wide format in Batch Workshop, or it may be exported in the Elliptic Fourier Analysis window.

2D Outlines:
All outlines created in a session may be exported as black silhouettes on a white background. Outlines are automatically filled prior to export.
All generated outlines will be exported unless they are manually removed in the Edit window.
Select a destination folder for export, a file type, and desired canvas size.
Scale: Outline area will be resized such that the filled shape covers 25% of the canvas. 
	   Some elongated shapes are exceptions to this. If an elongated shape at target area reaches past the margins, it is shrunk until it fits within the canvas.
Align: Outlines may be rotated onto the long axis to align orientations, or exported in original orientation.
